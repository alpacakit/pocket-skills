# Worked Examples

Each example demonstrates a distinct boundary and introduces no rules beyond the owning files.

## 1. Collapse many questions and compare on one frame

**Before:** "Budget ceiling? City or countryside? Overnight? Projector? Friday? Partners? Please advise."

The agenda, attendee form, and policy settle most items. Overnight versus day-only determines the rest, and three criteria could change the owner's preference.

**State:** Twelve venues fit 40 people in October. Nine fit the prior per-person limit. Two people cannot attend overnight because of fixed care commitments.

**Decision:** Choose Overnight or Day-only. You own the attendance and budget tradeoff. Venue holds expire Thursday; changing after deposit loses 20%.

**Options — fixed frame:**

| Criterion | Overnight | Day-only |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance coverage, event date; higher is better | 38 can attend | 40 can attend |
| Agenda coverage, event date; higher is better | Full agenda including evening session | Day agenda; evening session removed |
| Quoted cash cost; lower is better | JPY 1.4 million | JPY 600,000 |

**Recommendation:** Day-only.

**Reason:** The priority order is attendance coverage, then agenda coverage, then cash within the approved limit.

**Falsifier:** If preserving the evening session matters more than two absences, Day-only is wrong and Overnight is right.

**Evidence:** Verified: Tuesday's written quotes, availability, and attendee constraints, valid through Thursday. Inferred: the evening session improved prior outcomes, from two retrospective notes. Recommendation confidence: close.

**Next:** On either answer, I return the three best matching venue candidates today.

## 2. Perform the obvious action before asking

**Before:** "The supplier raised prices 12%. Should we accept, negotiate, or switch?"

Negotiation is a bounded check, not an owner-held option. Perform it first; then present only the live tradeoff.

**State:** Negotiation is complete. The incumbent will hold current prices for six months in exchange for a twelve-month commitment. An alternative is 8% below the new price but needs six weeks of qualification.

**Decision:** Choose Commit, Qualify, or No commitment. The procurement director owns the budget and signature. The price hold expires Friday; signing is hard to reverse.

**Objection:** Quality prefers Qualify to add a second source, but its six-week estimate is not an approved plan.

**Options:**

- **Commit** — Gain: avoids qualification and holds price. Sacrifice: twelve months of flexibility. Owner cost: the contractual commitment.
- **Qualify** — Gain: adds a cheaper second source. Sacrifice: delays audit preparation. Owner cost: six weeks of quality-team capacity.
- **No commitment** — Gain: preserves flexibility. Sacrifice: March unit price rises 12%. Owner cost: total increase is not measured while quantity remains open.

**Recommendation:** Commit.

**Reason:** The recommendation assumes the audit cannot move without penalty.

**Falsifier:** If the audit can move without penalty, Commit is wrong and Qualify is right.

**Evidence:** Verified: written terms and Friday expiry. Estimated: six weeks, from the quality lead. Not measured: March total and single-source risk. Recommendation confidence: conditional on the audit constraint.

**Next:** On Commit, I prepare a signer brief. On Qualify, I draft the qualification and audit-impact plan. On No commitment, I calculate the increase when quantity is final. I do not sign or send a commitment without separate execution authority.

## 3. Answer the original question when investigation moves the frame

**Before:** "Which candidate should we hire?" — "Neither; keep looking."

That answer skips the original comparison and hides the higher decision exposed by investigation.

**State:** Of the two candidates, B is stronger on the role's stated primary criterion. Interviews also indicate that coordination may be the lasting core of the job, but the role and screening process do not test it.

**Decision:** Choose Hire B or Rewrite and restart. You own the headcount allocation and schedule. B needs an answer Friday; restarting probably loses B, while a mistaken hire is expensive to reverse.

**Options:**

- **Hire B** — Gain: fills the seat next month. Sacrifice: leaves the role mismatch open. Owner cost: the risk and recovery cost of a mistaken hire.
- **Rewrite and restart** — Gain: aligns screening with the apparent job. Sacrifice: probably loses B. Owner cost: about two more months with the seat open.

**Recommendation:** Rewrite and restart.

**Reason:** The recommendation assumes coordination is a lasting core of the role.

**Falsifier:** If the coordination load is temporary, Rewrite and restart is wrong and Hire B is right.

**Evidence:** Verified: scores, comments, and B's Friday deadline. Inferred: coordination is a lasting requirement. Estimated: two months, from the normal search cycle. Recommendation confidence: conditional on the inferred requirement.

**Next:** On Hire B, I prepare an offer package for the authorized sender. On Rewrite and restart, I draft the role and a pause notice for approval.

## 4. Report completion when no judgment remains

**Before:** "Should I rank caterers by price or variety, include delivery, and check dietary requirements?"

The confirmed brief settles every item, so do not manufacture a decision ask.

**State:** Three caterers meet every dietary requirement, include delivery, and remain within the approved budget.

**Decided:** Chose North Kitchen because it provides the same required coverage at the lowest measured delivered price. Reversal costs nothing until an order is authorized.

**Evidence:** Verified against current menus, allergen sheets, and comparable delivered quotes for the same headcount and date.

**Next:** The ranked shortlist is ready. No judgment is requested and no order has been placed.
