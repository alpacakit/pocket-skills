# Evidence and Confidence

Label only claims that can change the decision. Do not make the whole report sound equally certain.

## Label claims

| Label | Meaning | Required context |
|---|---|---|
| **verified** | Directly checked against a named observation or source that supports the claim | Source or method, covered scope, and relevant freshness |
| **inferred** | Derived from verified evidence but not directly observed | Supporting evidence and inference step |
| **unchecked** | Checkable but not checked | Required check and why it matters |
| **currently unknowable** | Not checkable with current access, evidence, or timing | What must change before it becomes knowable |

These labels describe support, not guaranteed truth. A verified source can still be incomplete, stale, or unreliable; state that limitation when it matters. Never extend a check beyond its scope: one exercised case does not prove universal behavior, and "verified once" does not mean "true now."

## Label numbers

Give every decision-bearing number one status:

- **Measured:** value, reproducing method, scope, and time.
- **Estimated:** inputs, assumptions, and a useful range.
- **Not measured:** say so; do not substitute a plausible value.

These are number statuses, not additional evidence labels. Carry the status whenever the number is repeated, and reconcile totals with their stated parts or report the gap.

## Keep evidence honest

- Put an unchecked premise beside the recommendation when the recommendation depends on it.
- Put report-wide limits such as "no tests were run" in a short visible boundary, never under "for reference."
- Treat agreement as a convergence signal, not evidence about the world.
- Do not count repeated use of one source as corroboration.
- Treat a passing check as evidence only for what it exercised.
- Attribute inherited evidence to its source. A summary or briefing is not your measurement; re-measure before treating it as yours. Claim actions only when an independent record shows you performed them.

## State recommendation confidence separately

State the call separately from the facts beneath it:

- **Strong evidence, close call:** facts are solid; preference decides.
- **Thin evidence, conditionally clear call:** one unchecked premise controls the winner; identify the useful check.
- **Strong evidence, clear winner:** move the item to **Decide** if no value, risk, resource, or authority question remains.

This separation tells the owner whether to challenge evidence, supply a preference, or let a routine decision stand.
